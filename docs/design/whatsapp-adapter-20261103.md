## One page: the design as decided

**What it is.** WhatsApp as another door into a city's mayor, the way Slack, Google Chat and WeCom are today: the people who run a business talk to their own Dynasty's mayor on WhatsApp. Not a sales or marketing channel; Z and Milena sell and support the Brazilian deals and never appear in the channel. **The shape, in three sentences.** One Dynasty business number on Meta's official Cloud API, held in Afik's Meta business account, shared by every customer city, saved by owners as "Dynasty" with a plain photo; a gateway (a Cloudflare Worker at **whatsapp.dynasty.computer** in the Houmanoids Cloudflare account, written fresh in the fork on the contract Robert's relay proved, grown from a dumb relay into the key holder) verifies Meta's signature, maps each registered owner's phone to their city, hands that city its messages over the per-city credential the gateway minted at enrollment, and makes every Graph API call itself to send each city's replies on its behalf. On every box a `whatsapp` pack from the standard packs fork, built on the WeCom pack's shape, delivers inbound to the mayor through gc's extmsg with the owner's name, takes media and voice notes in (transcribed), and publishes the mayor's replies; inside WhatsApp's 24-hour window the mayor answers freely with read receipts and reactions, and after a quiet day it may write first only with one Meta-approved utility template and only when there is an actual update (Afik); the template does not reopen the window, only the owner's next message does. One-to-one chats first, exactly Instinct's WhatsApp today; the owner-and-team group that Taylor wants follows on the same number once Dynasty holds an Official Business Account, which Meta's Groups API requires.

## The number: one per city, or one shared (the decision)

The facts: Meta's Cloud API binds a webhook and a send token to a business phone number inside a Meta Business portfolio; a number cannot also be on anyone's WhatsApp app; an unverified portfolio holds 2 numbers and may message 250 new people a day outside the window; verification (CNPJ documents for a Brazilian entity) lifts both. Owner-to-mayor traffic is tiny, so limits never bind; what differs is who holds the Meta account and the secrets.

| | One number per city (Robert's model today) | One shared Dynasty number, routed (the Instinct model) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | the owner's business opens a Meta account, brings a +55 number not on WhatsApp, pastes a callback URL and token; a week of paperwork, Z or Milena walking them through | "send a WhatsApp to this number"; nothing in Meta for the owner |
| What the owner sees | their own business name and number | Dynasty's name and number |
| Where the Meta secrets live | on the owner's box; the box verifies Meta's signature itself; the ingress never holds a secret that can send | in our gateway; the gateway verifies, routes by the owner's phone to their city, and sends on each city's behalf; the box authenticates to the gateway with the per-city credential minted at enrollment |
| Blast radius | a ban or a lost token hits one city | hits every city at once |
| Privacy | the owner's traffic passes through our relay, and a relay that terminates HTTPS reads every webhook body: Meta's signature authenticates the plaintext JSON and encrypts nothing, so a relay holding no app secret keeps nothing from its operator; what stays with the owner is the app secret and the token, so only their box verifies and sends | every owner's message is readable at our gateway, which also holds the app secret and the access token and verifies the signature; the difference between the columns is who holds the secrets and verifies, not whether bodies can be read |
| Owner with two cities | two numbers, no ambiguity | needs a rule (one city per phone, or a prefix) |
| Fits Robert's running code | yes, as is | no: the ingress grows a routing table, a send proxy and the Meta secrets; the box adapter shrinks |
| Groups (Taylor's primary use) | needs an Official Business Account PER CUSTOMER, which a Brazilian small business will not get | one Dynasty OBA creates a group per city; the owner's team joins by invite link |

Both can coexist: shared by default, own number as an upgrade, behind one adapter with two ingress modes. The founders' pick is batch 2 question 1; the Groups gate (above) is the strongest fact on the table.

## Groups (Taylor's primary use: the mayor in a WhatsApp group with the owner and team)

Meta's own pages, read 11/3 (developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/groups/ and …/groups/groups-messaging/):

- "The Groups API is now open to all businesses with an Official Business Account (OBA)." Not available to numbers on the WhatsApp Business app. An OBA is the blue-badge tier (green until 2024) above ordinary business verification. How Dynasty gets one, per vendor readings of Meta's Official Business Accounts page (11/3): the number must be on the Business Platform for at least 30 days in good standing, the owning business portfolio verified, the display name matching the legal business name, two-factor on, the Messaging Policy kept; the request is filed in WhatsApp Manager and takes days; no fee on the Platform (the paid Meta Verified subscription is the path for the Business APP, not the API). Notability is still Meta's discretion, "at this time" on a rejection, and reapplying is allowed. So the earliest the Hou Dynasty's group can exist is about a month after the Dynasty number goes live, and only if Meta grants the badge; a Brazilian small business buying a Dynasty would never get one, which is the reason for the shared number.
- "Max group participants: 8." "Max groups you can create: 10,000 per business number." "Max Cloud API businesses per group: 1." So one group holds the business number plus up to seven people: the owner and a small team, which fits the use; a bigger team needs several groups.
- "Groups are an invite-only experience where participants join using a group invite link you send them." The BUSINESS creates the group (subject, optional description), gets a `group_lifecycle_update` webhook with the invite link, and sends the link by an approved "group invite link" template; each join fires a `group_participants_update` webhook. Nobody can add the business number to an existing family-style group; the city makes the group and invites the team.
- Messaging: `recipient_type: group`, `to: <group id>`; text, media, text and media templates; pins. Not supported: interactive messages, calling, disappearing and view-once messages, editing or deleting a message. Inbound group messages arrive on the messages webhook with a `group` identifier, so the adapter can key a `room` conversation by group id the way the wecom pack keys a chatid.
- Pricing and the window, from Meta's Groups pricing page (…/groups/pricing/, read 11/3): "you are charged each time a billable message is delivered to someone in the group", at each recipient's country rate; when any member messages the business "a customer service window is opened between you and the entire group" (24 hours, refreshed by each member message); since 2026-10-01 a free-form reply in an open window is free only within the number's 1,000 free service messages a month, "shared with 1:1 service messages for the same business phone number", one unit per delivered recipient; utility templates are billable even inside the window and "not eligible for volume tiers". So a group of the owner plus four staff, chatting daily, burns the free tier at five units a reply: about 200 mayor replies a month free, then about R$0.035 per recipient. A quiet day closes the window for the whole group; writing first then takes a template, as one-to-one does.

What it means for the design: (1) Robert's adapter (one-to-one only) needs a groups half: create, invite, `room` conversations, group status webhooks; gc already has the `room` kind. (2) The OBA gate pushes hard toward the shared Dynasty number: one Houmanoids or Dynasty OBA creates one group per customer city and invites that owner's team, while per-city numbers would need an OBA per Brazilian customer. (3) The invite flow is the onboarding: "the mayor sends you a link, you tap it, your team joins."

## The identity model

The owner (and anyone the owner names: a manager, a partner) is an allowlisted sender; each allowed number is one `dm` conversation `whatsapp/<digits>` bound to the mayor by the default route; the sender's WhatsApp profile name rides into gc as the actor's display name. Nobody outside the allowlist ever reaches the mayor (the gateway answers one canned line once, then drops and counts). Z and Milena appear nowhere in the channel; their work is the sale and the owner's first week.

## The path

Inbound, in order (the gateway's hold, the box's write-ahead journal, the gc post, the receipt, the acknowledgement): Meta → the gateway (a Cloudflare Worker at whatsapp.dynasty.computer in the Houmanoids account, on the contract Robert's relay proved: D1, 7-day retention, 5,000 pending per city), which verifies Meta's signature over the raw bytes with the app secret, drops strangers, writes each registered owner's message to that owner's city's inbox in D1 (one row per wamid, so Meta's redelivery is one message) and answers Meta 200 only once that row is committed (a failed write answers 500 and nothing is acknowledged; Meta redelivers) → the gateway HOLDS that delivery until the box acknowledges it by id, and the box acknowledges only on terminal success (below), never on a pending receipt, so a message whose gc receipt is still pending stays in the gateway with its body and is re-read by every poll; The in-doubt resolution waits until the posted line is older than the gc post's client deadline plus a margin (30 seconds plus 10), so a request gc did receive has answered or lost its caller and an append gc had begun inside its lock has finished; a store so slow that an append outlives that wait is the one residual race, accepted and named here.
